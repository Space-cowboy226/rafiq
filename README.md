Rafiq (رفيق) is an operational digital identity platform designed to support real-time verification, crowd safety, and rapid field response during high-density events such as Hajj and Umrah.

Rafiq does not replace existing smart cards or digital IDs. Instead, it introduces a field-operational layer that activates digital identity in real time by linking identity to status, location, and response actions in one unified system.

The problem addressed by Rafiq is the lack of a single operational interface in the field. Despite the presence of smart cards and digital systems, data remains fragmented across multiple agencies, verification can be slow under pressure, and managing lost pilgrims, medical incidents, or crowd congestion often relies on disconnected workflows. This creates operational gaps that delay decision-making and increase risk in peak-density environments.

Rafiq solves this by providing a real-time operational identity layer that connects digital identity with live field data. Field officers can instantly verify identity using QR codes, view the pilgrim’s current status and location, receive alerts for incidents or geofence breaches, and take immediate action. Each role interacts with the system through a dedicated interface tailored to their operational needs.

The platform includes identity verification through QR scanning, live monitoring of locations and alerts, operational dashboards with KPIs, and role-based views for campaign administrators, field security officers, health staff, pilgrims, and central operations. All interfaces are designed to be mobile-first, Arabic-native, and suitable for high-pressure field use.

From a technical perspective, this repository represents a functional prototype. The frontend is built using HTML, CSS, and vanilla JavaScript, with Leaflet used for maps and html5-qrcode for QR scanning. Data is simulated through mock REST endpoints and stored locally for demonstration purposes. The system is intended as an MVP to validate the concept rather than a production deployment.

Rafiq is designed with long-term impact in mind. It aims to reduce identity verification time from minutes to seconds, improve lost-pilgrim resolution, enhance crowd control, and strengthen coordination between security and health services. While the primary use case is Hajj, the platform is adaptable to other large-scale events such as Umrah, airports, stadiums, and national events.

Future development includes integration with official government identity APIs, biometric verification, augmented-reality navigation for crowd guidance, AI-assisted anomaly detection, and full zero-trust security enforcement with role-based access control.

This project was developed as part of an innovation and hackathon challenge. All data used in this repository is simulated. No real personal or governmental data is included.

Rafiq represents a shift from digital identity as a static credential to digital identity as a live operational tool.
